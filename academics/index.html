One degree. Many possibilities.

Every programme extends beyond one field. Engineers learn business and social science. Designers learn technology and human behaviour. Business students learn technology and ethics—so every student develops a broad, future-ready mindset.

Practice from the first year

Career preparation begins from the first year and includes portfolio development, mentorship, industry exposure through immersions, internships, capstone projects, and structured career guidance.

Research in the curriculum

Students can engage with research institutes and centres through projects, data, design, experimentation, and prototyping.

AI with judgement

AI is treated as a co-pilot, not a shortcut. Students are explicitly trained in AI literacy, how to use AI tools for research, prototyping, analysis, and ideation while being assessed on originality, reasoning, and reflection rather than raw output.

Assessment beyond rote outputs

Assessment uses oral defences, critiques, portfolios, and public presentations so students demonstrate reasoning, decision-making, and application—not just AI-assisted answers.

Ethics in context

Ethics is embedded through Grand Challenges with real stakeholders and the Active Citizenship Programme, connecting technological decisions with their social, moral, governance, and policy consequences.
