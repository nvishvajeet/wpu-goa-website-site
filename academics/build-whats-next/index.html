Build What's Next

Transdisciplinary learning for problems that do not fit within one subject.

Problems cross disciplines

The present is not divided into subjects. It is made up of problems. Complex. Interconnected. Human. The kind that cannot be solved by a single discipline.

This is what it means to be transdisciplinary. Not learning more subjects. But learning how to bring them together.

  • New ideas
  • New systems
  • Art, design, and media
  • Personal development
  • New technologies
  • New futures
  • New perspectives
  • New careers

Convergence in practice

Familiar systems work because knowledge, design, and human behaviour meet.

The Smartphone

A single device that combines computing, communication, photography, and design used by billions. Built through engineering, software, materials science, design, and human psychology. Not a discipline. A convergence.

Digital payments

How do you make money-transfer instant, secure, and usable by a vegetable vendor and a banker alike? This was built through banking systems, cybersecurity, mobile design, public infrastructure and behavioural trust. Systems only work when people trust them.

Recommendation systems

How do platforms understand what you might want to watch, read, or listen to next? This was built through AI, data science, storytelling and human behaviour. Predicting choices is both technical and human.

Urban mobility

Ride-sharing platforms combine computer science, GPS engineering, behavioural psychology, business models, and intuitive design to address inefficient and unsafe city transport.

Personal health

Wearable fitness trackers combine sensor engineering, data analytics, health sciences, industrial design, and motivational psychology to support healthier habits.

Modular shelter

Student-designed modular shelters combine architecture, materials science, cultural studies, engineering, and user-centred design to address urgent housing needs.

Questions worth working on

Students examine problems whose technical and human dimensions cannot be separated.

Learning that lasts

Education rewards memory, not understanding. Solving this requires cognitive science, learning design, AI systems, and motivational psychology.

Preventive health

We wait for illness instead of preventing it. Solving this requires biology, data science, wearable tech, and behaviour change.

Digital wellbeing

Every system competes for your focus. Solving this requires neuroscience, media design, algorithms, and ethics.

Career readiness

Solving this requires industry integration, skill design, behavioural training, and new assessment systems.

Liveable cities

Growth does not always mean better living. Solving this requires urban design, mobility systems, data infrastructure, and policy thinking.

Creative technology

We divide what should be combined. Solving this requires design, AI tools, media, and cultural understanding.

What students develop

  • Navigate complexity with clarity
  • Create new possibilities
  • Adapt continuously
